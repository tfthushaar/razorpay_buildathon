Real finding: 100% success rate at every concurrency level tested, 1 through 32 concurrent
requests, zero errors. Mean latency degrades gracefully, not catastrophically -- roughly 2.2x
higher at 32x the concurrency (2.157s -> 4.750s), not an exponential or cliff-edge failure. This
doesn't mean single-instance SQLite scales indefinitely -- it means the specific claim "this
architecture would fall over under any real concurrent load" isn't supported by what was actually
measured, up to the range tested here. Worker-pool narration and a move to Postgres remain real,
legitimate next steps for load well beyond this range, but are deferred as genuinely unmeasured-as-
necessary rather than built speculatively against a problem not shown to exist yet at this scale.
